NEST is led by Bart Ellenbroek and Jiun Youn, with a growing group of researchers and students. Full biographies below.

Bart Ellenbroek

Group leader

Bart obtained his PhD in 1988 from Radboud University Nijmegen in the Netherlands and, after working in several countries, joined Victoria University of Wellington in 2012. Trained as a behavioural neuroscientist and pharmacologist, his research initially focused on developing animal models for psychiatric disorders such as schizophrenia, drug addiction and autism spectrum disorders.

More recently he has become fascinated by how the brain — and behaviour — change over time. This has led him to investigate neurodevelopment in animals and humans, and how drugs such as alcohol and psychedelics change our brain and behaviour. His work spans animal research (rats and planaria) and research in humans.

Acknowledging the enormous complexity of the brain, he is a strong proponent of collaboration and has established many past and ongoing collaborations. Outside the lab he is married to Jiun Youn, and has two daughters, one son, two dogs and two budgies.

Alex Lister

PhD Student & Research Assistant

Hi, I'm Alex, a PhD candidate and Research Assistant in the NEST. I pitch in to help students wherever asked (usually in the biology lab or writing feedback), while my own work focuses on how maternal inflammation during early/mid-pregnancy may impact the foetoplacental regulation of serotonin.

Serotonin has its sticky fingers in the activity of everything from microglia to mitochondria, so changes in prenatal serotonin availability may have a plethora of downstream neurodevelopmental implications. This includes in the symptomology and aetiology of diagnoses such as schizophrenia spectrum, autism spectrum, and depressive disorders.

There's plenty of PhD's worth of research left to do in this area - if you're looking for something interesting to research, I can recommend it and am happy to chat about it!

Mengqi Zhang

PhD Student

Hi I am Mengqi I joined NEST in the middle of 2025. I am a PhD researcher working on the effects of psychedelic compounds on neurogenesis, using planarians as a model system.

My research focuses on molecular and cellular mechanisms related to drug development, rather than behavioral studies. I have strong laboratory experience and work mainly with molecular biology, immunohistochemical and pharmacological techniques. I am interested in how basic neuroscience research can support drug discovery.

Sasha Middleton

Student Researcher

Hi! I’m Sasha a fourth-year psychology student finishing off my BPsyc, majoring in Brain Science and Mental Health! As part of my SCIE306 course, I am very excited to work on a research project investigating the addictive properties of methamphetamine in planaria. As part of the project, I will be optimizing a computer program designed to automatically analyse activity, shapes and behavior of planaria.

A huge range of psychological research interests me, but learning about neurobiology and gene-environment interactions, especially the impact of the gut microbiome, especially piques my passion. This year, I am also doing an internship under the Tindall Foundation utilising existing kaupapa Māori and/or Pasifika frameworks and methodologies to support the mental well-being of birthing parents in their perinatal phase.
